# Zombies module: architecture and debug switches

The Zombies example (`examples/zombies_module`) is a top-down shooter built on the engine as a game module
(`ZombiesModule.dll` on Windows, linked statically into `libpakal.so` on Android). The engine stays generic; everything
about this game lives in the module.

## Levels

- **Downtown** (`downtown_survival_01`, `downtown_survival_02`): a city made with Quaternius's CC0 packs (see
  [ZOMBIES_DOWNTOWN.md](ZOMBIES_DOWNTOWN.md)). Generated by `tools/zombies_convert/build_downtown.py`.

Downtown takes the game rules (weapons, enemies, gift tables), the sounds and the shot effects from a level converted from
the original game's data. That data is not free and is not part of this repository; the converters in
`tools/zombies_convert` need a local copy of it.

## Architecture

The module is built on the TopDown module (`game_genres/topdown`, see [TOPDOWN_MODULE.md](TOPDOWN_MODULE.md)): the actors,
the generic components (health, the player's body, control and weapon, consumables, props, destructibles, the gift box,
pickups, escorts), the spawners, the enemy movement and the views (character models, props, effects, sounds) are the
module's. What is this game's own:

- `ZombiesModule.cpp`: exports the application that loads the level (`ZOMBIES_LEVEL`, or the default one).
- `GameRules`: the TopDown rules plus the enemy types, read from the `rules` and `destructibles` sections of `level.json`
  (the original game's object library: weapons, player, enemy types with their berserk tables, money, the gift table and
  its consumables, props with health, drops and explosions). What the data leaves out is the original's (the dual Glock,
  its sounds, money on the floor).
- `ZombiesGame`: the simulation, without windows or drawing, the `TopDownGame` of this game. Owns the actors; `step()`
  runs the components, bullets as projectiles (they stop at a wall, a zombie or a prop; zombies are hit on a circle as wide
  as their model, see `EnemyRules::hit_radius`), explosions, drops, and the counters the scripts read. Its own: berserk,
  money and tokens (and the zombies that take money from the floor), barricades, escorts, the air strike.
- `Components.h/.cpp`: the zombie brain (wraps TopDown's `EnemyAI2D`, with the special attacks of the boomer, the tourist,
  the dog walker, the dogs, the zombinator and the bear). It tells the character view how it looks (`CharacterViewState`).
- `TopDown.h`: the module's names are used in `Zombies` without the namespace.
- `ZombieScript`: the original game's script functions (`SetEnemySpawn`, `ShowAutoDialog`, `FadeOut`...) on the engine's
  `ScriptHost`. Each objective hook is a coroutine; `Wait(ms)` yields to the game clock.
- `ZombiesLevelState`: plays one level. It coordinates; the parts are in files of their own:
  - `ZombiesLoading.cpp`: the loading in steps under the loading screen (document, rules, models, objects, physics through
    TopDown's `build_level_physics`, the player, zombie pools, scripts, light, camera). The model files load in the
    background (the engine's `AssetCache`): all are asked for at once, decode on its workers, and upload 40 ms of each
    frame. A level hands its cache and models to the next level it changes to (a restart, the next mission), so a
    restart loads almost nothing.
  - `ZombiesScripting.cpp`: what each script function does on the level, and the objectives in order.
  - `ZombiesHud.h/.cpp`: the HUD and the panel over the game (it shows a `HudView`; the level does what its buttons say).
  - `WorldOverlays.h/.cpp`: what is drawn in the world (health bars, markers, beacons, the aiming cursor, colliders).
  - `ZombiesDevPanel.cpp`: the development panel (F1).
  - `AutoplayBot.h/.cpp`: the bot of `ZOMBIES_AUTOPLAY`.
  - The camera, the input and the building fade are TopDown's (`CameraRig`, `InputMapper`, `OccluderFade`).

The simulation runs in fixed steps of 1/60 s (`SimulationClock`). Characters and the camera are drawn between the last
step and the next (`Actor::drawn_position`), so movement stays smooth whatever the frame rate. The same seed
(`ZOMBIES_SEED`, through `RandomStreams`) plays the same game: the bot's runs are checked against stored traces
(`tools/bot_check.ps1`, see `tests/baseline/README.md`), eight times faster than real time (`ZOMBIES_TIMESCALE`).

Controls: WASD moves (camera-relative), the character faces the mouse, the left button fires (arrows, IJKL to aim and Space to
fire also work). `R` restarts, `P` pauses, `F3` shows the colliders. On Android: two virtual sticks (left moves, right aims and
fires past 41% of its travel) and the top center square restarts (see [ANDROID.md](ANDROID.md)).

## Campaign and progress

- The game opens on its menu (`MenuState`): play (the missions in order), shop. `ZOMBIES_LEVEL` skips it and plays one level.
- `examples/zombies_module/game/campaign.json` (copied to `Assets/game`): the missions (level, reward for the first win and for
  replays, three stars: win, end with some health, win in time or with enough kills), the guns (the Downtown ones, lent by the
  chest in a level, are bought here and carried from the start), upgrades (damage and fire rate per level), toughness (more
  health) and boosts carried into a mission (used from the HUD). One currency, earned by playing: money picked up stays even
  when a mission is lost; a win pays the mission's reward and opens the next one. No premium currency, no waiting.
- Script functions for missions: `DropPickup(pickup, point)` (9002 is a token: it stays until taken and counts in
  `pickupCount`), `Fail(text)` (the mission is lost: the panel says why, the game stops), `IsPlayerNear(point, radiusCm)`,
  `ShowMarker(point, on)` (a beacon; an arrow by the player points to the nearest beacon, or else the nearest token).
- Options (OPTIONS on the main menu, and in the pause panel): the picture's quality (AUTO is the device's default) and FSR
  upscaling (on at first: the picture drawn at 67% and rebuilt with FSR 1; off: at the screen's size), and the look: CLEAN
  or SKETCH (the town as an etching on warm paper: pen strokes along each surface's plane, more and crossed in the dark,
  a speckled grain; `BgfxGraphicsSystem::set_sketch`, in the post-process pass). Saved in the profile
  (`options`) and given to the graphics at the start (`Session::apply_options`, `BgfxGraphicsSystem::set_fsr`).
- Heroes: the kit's four survivors (Sam, Lis, Matt, Shaun; `heroes` in the campaign). Sam is there from the start; the others
  are rescued in side missions (`"side": true, "unlocks": "<hero>"`: a side mission opens with the one before it and the
  next ones do not wait for it). The HEROES screen picks the one played (`Profile::hero`); the level dresses the player with
  `visuals.heroes[<hero>]`, and an escort mission dresses its survivor the same way. The kit's characters come holding a
  weapon (a rigid piece on a hand bone): the converter leaves it out (`KIT_HELD_WEAPONS`) and the look's `held` pistol, or the
  weapon lent, goes in the hand. Portraits: `tools/blender/hero_portraits.py`.
- Both hands on the gun: the look's `reach` arm (the kit's right arm: shoulder, elbow, knuckles) bends by two-bone IK
  (`MeshComponent3D_Bgfx::set_reach`, after the clip's pose) so the hand takes the weapon's `grip` (a point of the weapon's
  model, held by the other hand): always on a long gun (`twoHanded`), on the pistol while it fires; not while reloading or
  swinging. The kit's arms are short, so the grip is on the barrel near the hand that holds it.
- Defend and escort in the game: `StartEscort(hero, start, point...)` makes the survivor (`EscortComponent`; the game variables
  `escortState` 0 waiting, 1 walking, 2 arrived, 3 dead, and `escortHealth` 0-100); props with a `Barricade` component are
  gates the player repairs by standing next to them, one with `Objective` is what the zombies go for
  (`EnemyBrainComponent::choose_victim`).
- The profile is saved to `save/profile.json` (under the app's storage on Android) after every change. Logic and tests:
  `Meta.h/.cpp`, `tests/MetaTests.cpp`.

## Interface

- **Game interface**: RmlUi (`source/bgfx/BgfxRmlUi`, the engine's `IUIManager`). Documents and style sheets in
  `examples/zombies_module/ui` (copied to `Assets/ui`): `hud.rml` is the HUD and the pause / down / level complete panel,
  `zombies.rcss` its look (sizes in `dp`: 1 dp is a pixel at 720 lines). The game changes elements by id
  (`set_text`, `set_class`, `set_property`) and hears clicks by id. Fonts: Roboto and Bangers (`ui/fonts/README.md`).
- **Development panels**: Dear ImGui (`source/bgfx/BgfxDebugUI`), F1 or three fingers on a phone; `PAKAL_DEV_UI=1` shows
  them from the start. The Zombies panel: frame rate, level list and restart, god mode, money, consumables, spawn any
  enemy type, and Graphics: quality level, post-process, SSAO, tilt-shift, real-time shadows, lightmap, light probes, the
  upscaler (FSR 1 or bilinear, and its sharpness) when the picture is drawn smaller than the screen, fading. On the
  desktop the picture is drawn at most `PAKAL_RENDER_HEIGHT` lines high (1080 by default, 0: the window's own): a
  taller window (a maximized one) is drawn smaller and scaled up. `PAKAL_RENDER_SCALE=0.67` draws at that share of the
  window instead (`PAKAL_UPSCALER=bilinear|fsr`, `PAKAL_SHARPNESS` in stops, `off` for no sharpening pass).
- Both draw in their own views after the 3D picture. On Android the game updates on another thread than the drawing:
  the interface locks its documents, and the Zombies panel locks the level's state.

## Character viewer and zombie encyclopedia

`CharacterViewer.h/.cpp`: one character at a time on a turntable, lit as the first level of the campaign is (its
`lighting` and `post`, through `LevelLook`), to pick a hero and to check that every model looks right. The menu opens it:
3D VIEW on the HEROES screen, ZOMBIEPEDIA on the main screen; `ZOMBIES_VIEWER=heroes|enemies` opens it directly.

- **Heroes** (the campaign's, with their story and whether they are rescued): every clip of the model, and what they can
  hold (empty hands, the gun, each weapon a consumable lends). The other hand reaching for a gun's grip (two-bone IK) is
  drawn as lines (shoulder, elbow, hand, and a cross at the grip; red when the hand is more than 3 cm off) with the
  distance written: `R` turns the reach off to compare.
- **Zombies** (`visuals.enemy` of the level): each type with its numbers from the rules (health, speed, damage, berserk,
  its attack), its clips and its model variants (`V`).

Its panels are an RmlUi document like the menus' (`ui/viewer.rml`, `ui/viewer.rcss`), over the model: on the left the
hero (portrait, story, whether it is rescued, PLAY AS) or the zombie (its numbers as bars against the strongest, on a
square-root scale, and its attack); on the right the clip, what it holds or which model, and the toggles (pause,
slower, faster, spin, closer, farther, other hand, lines); at the bottom the arrows between characters. Every button
works with a finger. Keys on a desktop: Left/Right character, Up/Down clip, Q/E weapon, V model, R other hand, L lines,
Space pause, Z/X slower and faster, A/D turn, W/S closer and farther, T spin, Esc back.

`ZOMBIES_VIEWER_ENTRY`, `ZOMBIES_VIEWER_WEAPON` and `ZOMBIES_VIEWER_CLIP` (a clip's name) start on one;
`ZOMBIES_VIEWER_CLICKS=v_next,v_second_next,...` presses its buttons one every half second; `ZOMBIES_VIEWER_LEVEL` reads
another level; `ZOMBIES_SCREENSHOT` and `ZOMBIES_SCREENSHOT_AT` take a capture as in the levels.

`ZOMBIES_VIEWER_SHOTS=<folder>` takes a capture of every weapon with each clip of `ZOMBIES_VIEWER_SHOT_CLIPS` (and logs
how far the other hand is from each grip), and `ZOMBIES_VIEWER_YAW=<radians>` holds the camera at one side (-1.57: the
hero's right): what the weapons were fixed with.

Weapons in the hands (fixed with it): the hero always holds a gun, so it stands, walks and runs with the kit's clips that
carry it in front (`Idle_Gun`, `Walk_Gun`, `Run_Gun`); with the arms down the guns pointed at the floor and the other hand
crossed the body for a long gun's grip. The guns keep the kit's own pose in the left hand. The melee weapons go in the
right hand (the animation library swings with it) at the kit's place mirrored, turned so they come out of the fist
forward. A two-handed gun rests in both hands (`gunIdle`, `Idle_Gun` by default; the viewer starts on it too): with the
plain idle it hung from one hand, its grip 27 cm out of the other's reach. The values are in
`tools/zombies_convert/build_downtown.py` (`KIT_MELEE_*`, `KIT_HERO_CLIPS`);
`tools/zombies_convert/update_holds.py` writes them into levels already built.

## Debug switches (environment variables)

On Android, put `KEY=VALUE` lines in `/sdcard/Android/data/org.pakalengine.zombies/files/pakal_env.txt`.

Engine:

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| `PAKAL_FPS_LOG=1` | One line per second: FPS of each system, and `[GPU]` with draws, triangles and models per pass |
| `PAKAL_GPU=discrete\|integrated\|nvidia\|amd\|intel\|any` | Which GPU on a computer with two (Direct3D picks the adapter by vendor; the default is the dedicated NVIDIA one, the log names the one in use) |
| `PAKAL_DEV_UI=1` | Shows the development panels (Dear ImGui) from the start; F1 toggles them |
| `PAKAL_RENDER_HEIGHT=<lines>` | Desktop: the most lines the picture is drawn at (1080 by default, 0: the window's); a taller window gets it scaled up by FSR. Android: the picture's height (720, 1080 at ultra) |
| `PAKAL_SHARPNESS=<stops>\|off` | FSR's sharpening (RCAS): stops below the most; `off` leaves its pass out |
| `ZOMBIES_AO=<strength>` | The screen's ambient occlusion (SSAO). A level with baked light (light probes, a lightmap) has it baked: without this variable SSAO runs only at ultra (the dev panel can turn it on) |
| `PAKAL_DEPTH_SORT=0` | Sends solid models by program instead of nearest first (to compare) |
| `PAKAL_FPS_CAP=<n>`, `PAKAL_VSYNC=0` | The picture's rate on the desktop player (60 by default, 0 = no cap); vsync off |
| `PAKAL_LOGIC_FPS=<n>`, `PAKAL_PHYSICS_FPS=<n>` | The game logic's and the physics' rates (120 by default; each system runs at its own rate, physics one step per update) |
| `PAKAL_RESOLUTION=WxH` | Window size of the desktop player |
| `PAKAL_RENDER_HEIGHT=<px>` | Android: height the game is drawn at (the compositor scales it to the screen) |
| `PAKAL_QUALITY=low\|medium\|high\|ultra` | Quality level (see ANDROID.md) |
| `PAKAL_BGFX_RENDERER` | Forces a bgfx renderer |
| `PAKAL_NO_CULL=1` | Draws back faces too |
| `PAKAL_MCP_PORT`, `PAKAL_MCP_TOKEN` | The desktop player's MCP server |

Zombies:

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| `ZOMBIES_LEVEL=<level.json>` | Level to load |
| `ZOMBIES_AUTOPLAY=1` / `idle` | A bot plays (shoots, keeps its distance, opens the gift, picks up money) / stands still |
| `ZOMBIES_AUTOMOVE=x,y`, `ZOMBIES_AUTOFIRE=1` | Holds a movement / keeps firing |
| `ZOMBIES_SEED=<n>` | Random seed |
| `ZOMBIES_VIEWER=heroes\|enemies` | Opens the character viewer or the zombie encyclopedia (see above) |
| `ZOMBIES_TIMESCALE=<x>` | Plays x times faster (more fixed steps per frame, the same game; at most 0.25 s of game per frame). `bot_check.ps1` uses 8 |
| `ZOMBIES_GIVE=<id>` | Starts with that consumable |
| `ZOMBIES_REACH=off\|x,y,z` | The other hand on the gun: off, or its grip at that point of the weapon's model (to fit `grip`) |
| `ZOMBIES_GLOSS=<k>` | The plastic toy highlight's strength (the level's `lighting.gloss`, 0.35 by default; 0 turns it off) |
| `ZOMBIES_SKETCH=<k>` | The sketch look at that strength (0.85 is the option's) |
| `ZOMBIES_HUD_CLICKS=<s>:<id>,...` | Clicks those HUD elements at those game times (`btn_revive`, `opt_fsr_off`...) |
| `ZOMBIES_HERO=sam\|lis\|matt\|shaun` | Plays as that hero (any level, even when not rescued) |
| `ZOMBIES_SPAWN=<type>:<alive>:<cooldownMs>,...` | More spawn orders on top of the script's (`25016:2:3000` keeps two boomers coming) |
| `ZOMBIES_START=x,y` | Warps go to that point (meters) |
| `ZOMBIES_DEBUG_KILL_EVERY=<s>` | Removes a living zombie at that interval |
| `ZOMBIES_DEBUG_BREAK=<m>` | Breaks every prop that close to the player 3 s into the game (to see their effects) |
| `ZOMBIES_SCREENSHOT=<file.tga>`, `ZOMBIES_SCREENSHOT_AT=<s>` | One capture at that time |
| `ZOMBIES_LIVE_RELOAD=1`, `ZOMBIES_EDIT=1`, `ZOMBIES_EDIT_SELECT=<key>` | The level is built again when its file changes; the level panel open, editing; an object selected (docs/LEVEL_EDITOR.md) |
| `ZOMBIES_PAUSE_AT=<s>` | Pauses at that time (to look at the pause panel) |
| `ZOMBIES_PROFILE=<file.json>` | Another profile file (tests) |
| `ZOMBIES_MENU_CLICKS=<id>,<id>,...`, `ZOMBIES_MENU_SHOT=<file.tga>` | Clicks menu elements one per half second (`nav_play,mission_0`), then captures the screen |
| `ZOMBIES_SCREENSHOT_LOADING=<file.tga>` | Captures the loading screen halfway |
| `ZOMBIES_NO_FADE=1` | Nothing fades (also in the development panel) |
| `ZOMBIES_SHOTS=<prefix>,<start>,<interval>,<count>` | A series of captures |
| `ZOMBIES_CAM_SCALE=<k>`, `ZOMBIES_CAM_AT=x,y`, `ZOMBIES_CAM_ON_ENEMY=1\|<type>` | Pulls the camera back / looks at a point / follows a zombie |
| `ZOMBIES_CLIP_TEST=<clip>` | Every character plays that clip |
| `ZOMBIES_ONLY=<text>` | Only the level models whose file contains the text |
| `ZOMBIES_SHOW_COLLIDERS=1` | Draws the colliders |
| `ZOMBIES_POST=on\|off`, `ZOMBIES_SHADOWS=on\|off`, `ZOMBIES_GI=off` | Post-process, sun shadows, baked ambient light |
| `ZOMBIES_AO=<strength>`, `ZOMBIES_TILT=<px>`, `ZOMBIES_POST_QUALITY=<0..1>`, `ZOMBIES_SHADOW_SIZE=<texels>` | Override the level's look |
